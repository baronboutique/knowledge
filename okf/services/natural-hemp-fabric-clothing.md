## The Himalayan Advantage: What Altitude Does to the Fiber

Hemp growing between 1,500m and 3,000m. The altitude is the reason the fiber behaves differently.

Most industrial hemp is grown on flat, low-level plains. Ours is cultivated between 1,500m and 3,000m in the Himalayas, and that environment changes the fiber in two measurable ways.

- **More lignin.** At altitude, plants face intense UV-B radiation. The hemp responds by producing more lignin, a natural polymer that armors the fiber, which is why alpine-grown hemp carries higher UV protection and durability than low-altitude hemp.
- **Denser cellulose.** Mountain wind and cold trigger thigmomorphogenesis, a growth response that builds thicker, more flexible stems with a higher density of crystalline cellulose. That is where the strength comes from.
- **Cleaner input.** Mineral-rich snowmelt and air without industrial pollution, which matters if your skin is sensitive.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is hemp fabric natural or synthetic?

Natural. Hemp is a plant fiber, in the same family of textiles as linen and cotton. Nothing petroleum-based goes into it, it sheds no microplastics when washed, and it is fully compostable in about six months. Polyester, by contrast, is plastic and takes 200 years or more to break down.

### What does hemp fabric actually feel like?

Close to linen. Handwoven Himalayan hemp has a soft, breathable texture with a slight irregularity in the surface that comes from being made on a loom rather than a machine. The thing people do not expect is that it improves: hemp softens with every wash, where synthetics deteriorate. A hemp shirt is at its best after a year, not on the day you buy it.

### Do you sell hemp clothing, or only the fabric?

Both, and they are the same cloth. You can buy fabric by the meter from 10 meters and make something yourself, or we can make the garment for you. Finished clothing is made to your measurements with no minimum, so a single shirt is a valid order.

### What hemp garments do you make?

Shirts and tunics, pants and bottoms, jackets and outerwear, and scarves and shawls, plus anything custom. Suits, dresses, a hemp T-shirt, a jumpsuit: if you can specify it, our tailors can make it. Custom garments are made to your measurements rather than picked from a size run.

### Do you make hemp T-shirts?

Not as a stock line, but yes as a custom garment. Tell us the fit, the weight you want and the neckline, and it is made to your measurements. For a brand ordering a run, hemp T-shirts fall under private label at 10 pieces per style.

### What makes Himalayan hemp different from other hemp fabric?

Altitude. Ours grows between 1,500m and 3,000m, where intense UV-B makes the plant produce more lignin, which armors the fiber, and where mountain wind and cold build denser crystalline cellulose in the stem. The result is measurably stronger and more UV-resistant than low-altitude industrial hemp. It is then hand-processed and handwoven rather than mechanically stripped, which preserves the softness that mass processing removes.

### Is hemp really better than cotton or linen?

On several measures, yes. It uses 50% less water than cotton, needs no pesticides, and produces fabric about three times stronger. Against linen it is more durable, softer over time, and offers better UV protection. Where cotton wins is familiarity and price. We would rather tell you that than pretend hemp is better at everything.

### How long does hemp last compared to synthetic fabric?

A well-made hemp garment can last 20 years or more with proper care, while synthetics typically deteriorate within 2 to 5. Hemp’s strength actually increases when wet, and the fabric improves with age rather than thinning. That makes it an investment piece rather than a disposable one.

### Is hemp clothing suitable for sensitive skin?

Yes. Hemp is naturally hypoallergenic and antimicrobial, and our chemical-free cultivation and processing means no pesticides, synthetic dyes or chemical finishes touch the fiber. People who react to synthetic fabrics often find hemp unusually comfortable.

### How do I care for hemp clothing and fabric?

Machine wash cold or hand wash with a gentle detergent. Air dry or tumble dry low. Avoid bleach and harsh chemicals. Hemp softens and improves with washing, and its natural antimicrobial properties mean it needs washing less often than most fabrics.
