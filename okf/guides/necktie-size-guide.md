# Necktie Size Guide: Length, Width, and How to Choose

Two numbers decide whether a tie sits right: how long it is, and how wide the blade is. Every Baron Boutique necktie is made 60 inches long, in three widths: skinny at 2 inches, standard at 3 inches, and extra large at 3.75 inches. Custom lengths and widths cost nothing, because every tie is cut after you order it. This guide explains what those numbers mean, how to measure them, and how to choose.

## How a necktie is measured

Three measurements describe any necktie. Once you know them, every size chart in the world becomes readable.

Length

Tip to tip along the center of the tie, laid flat and untwisted. This is the number that decides where the tie ends on your body.

Blade width, which we call the tie’s end

Measured straight across the widest point of the wide end. When anyone says a tie is 3 inches, this is what they mean.

Head width, which we call the tie’s head

Measured across the narrow end, the tip that finishes behind the blade. It is narrower than the blade on every classic tie.

Junction point

A silk tie is cut on the bias in separate panels, then joined. The junction point is where those panels meet. It is a construction landmark rather than a fitting one, but it tells you the tie was cut properly rather than stamped out in one straight piece.

## Which tie width should you choose?

The rule that matters more than any other: **match the blade to your lapel.** Measure the widest point of your jacket lapel and pick the tie width within about half an inch of it. A 3 inch tie against a 3.25 inch lapel looks considered. A 2 inch tie against a 4 inch lapel looks like an accident.

| Width | Blade | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skinny | 2 in | Narrow lapels, slim frames, shorter wearers, informal and evening looks where the tie is meant to read as a line rather than a statement. |
| Standard | 3 in | Most notch lapels, most builds, business and wedding wear. If you own one tie, own this width. |
| Extra Large | 3 3/4 in | Peak lapels, broad shoulders and chest, taller wearers, and anyone who wants the tie to carry visual weight against a heavier cloth. |

Build matters too, because a tie is read against the width of your chest. As a rough guide, slimmer frames sit well between 2.25 and 2.75 inches, average and athletic frames between 2.5 and 3.25 inches, and broader frames between 3 and 3.5 inches. Our three stock widths bracket that range, and anything in between can be cut for you.

If we are making the jacket as well, tell us and we will cut the tie to the lapel we are cutting for you. That is the one advantage of ordering both from the same workshop.

## How long should a necktie be?

**The tip of the blade should end at the middle of your belt buckle**, or at the top of your waistband if you are not wearing a belt. That is the whole target. Everything else is arithmetic working back from it.

Most ties on the market are 57 to 58 inches. We make ours at 60 inches as standard, which covers most wearers up to roughly 6 feet 2 inches with a four-in-hand or a half Windsor. Four things eat into that length:

- **Height.** A longer torso needs a longer tie. Above about 6 feet 2 inches, ask for 62 to 64 inches.
- **The knot.** A full Windsor uses roughly 4 to 6 inches more silk than a four-in-hand.
- **Build.** A fuller chest and waist means the tie travels further before it hangs free.
- **Where your trousers sit.** A low waistband puts the buckle further down, so the tie has to reach further. A high-rise waistband needs a shorter tie, not a longer one.

If two of these apply to you at once, add length. It is free, and a tie that is slightly long can be absorbed by a bigger knot, while a tie that is short cannot be fixed at all.

## How much length does your knot use?

| Knot | Length used | Pairs with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Four-in-hand | Least | Any width. Slightly asymmetric, which is part of its character. The safe choice with a skinny tie or a narrow collar. |
| Half Windsor | A little more | Standard width and most collars. Symmetrical without being heavy. |
| Full Windsor | About 4 to 6 in more than a four-in-hand | Standard or extra large width with a spread collar. Ask for extra length if you tie this every day. |

Cloth weight compounds this. A three-fold silk tie with a wool interlining, which is how ours are built, ties a fuller knot than a thin lined tie of the same width, so allow for it rather than being surprised by it.

## How to measure a tie you already own

The fastest way to order a tie that fits is to measure one that already does.

1. Lay the tie flat on a table, untwisted, seam side down. Do not stretch it.
2. Run a tape along the center from the tip of the wide end to the tip of the narrow end. That is the length.
3. Measure straight across the widest point of the wide end. That is the width.
4. Send us both numbers. If your favorite tie ends exactly where you want it, we will match that length rather than guess from your height.

Bias-cut silk stretches slightly under tension. Measure it relaxed, not pulled taut, or you will order a tie an inch shorter than you meant to.

## What our neckties are made of

Construction changes how a tie knots and how it hangs, so it belongs in a sizing guide rather than a separate story.

- 100 percent satin silk at 18.5 momme, about 80 grams per square meter.
- Cut on the bias at 45 degrees, which is what lets the tie hang straight and recover its shape rather than twisting.
- Three-fold construction, interlined in wool and lined in silk.
- Hand slip stitched, self-tipped, with a bar tack on the blade.
- Handmade to order by our tailors in Kathmandu.

The wool interlining is the part that matters for sizing. It gives the knot body, which is what makes a small four-in-hand hold its shape, and it is also why the knot takes a little more silk than a lightweight tie would.

## Caring for a silk necktie

- Dry clean only. Silk and water do not forgive each other.
- Untie the knot by reversing the steps you used. Pulling the narrow end through the knot stretches the bias and shortens the life of the tie.
- Roll the tie loosely or hang it, and give it a day between wears so the bias can relax back.
- If it creases, hang it in a steamy bathroom rather than pressing it. A hot iron flattens the roll of the edges, which is the part a hand-finished tie is built for.

## Necktie sizing questions

### What is the standard necktie length?

Most neckties sold today are 57 to 58 inches long. Every Baron Boutique necktie is made at 60 inches, about 152 cm, in all three widths. The extra length gives you room for a larger knot or a taller frame without ordering a special size.

### Where should the tip of a necktie end?

At the middle of your belt buckle, or at the very top of your waistband if you are not wearing a belt. Ending above the buckle looks unfinished. Ending below it makes the whole outfit read as borrowed.

### What tie width should I wear?

Match the widest point of the blade to the widest point of your jacket lapel, within about half an inch. Our standard 3 inch blade suits most notch lapels. The 2 inch skinny suits narrow lapels and slimmer frames. The 3.75 inch extra large suits peak lapels, broad shoulders and taller wearers.

### Do you make extra long neckties?

Yes. Our standard is already 60 inches, and we will cut it longer at no extra cost. Tell us your height, the knot you tie, and where you want the tip to land, and we will work the length back from there.

### How do I measure a necktie?

Lay the tie flat and untwisted, seam side down. Measure along the center from the tip of the wide end to the tip of the narrow end for the length. Measure straight across the widest point of the wide end for the width. Those two numbers are all we need.

### Does a Windsor knot need a longer tie?

Yes. A full Windsor uses roughly 4 to 6 inches more than a four-in-hand, because it wraps the blade twice. If you tie a full Windsor and you are over 6 feet tall, ask us for extra length rather than living with a short blade.

### What is the difference between skinny, standard and extra large?

Only the blade width. Skinny is 2 inches, standard is 3 inches, extra large is 3.75 inches. All three are 60 inches long, and all three are cut from the same 18.5 momme satin silk with the same construction.

### What tie size suits a tall man?

Above roughly 6 feet 2 inches, or if you tie a full Windsor, ask for 62 to 64 inches. A wider blade also balances a longer torso, so the extra large width is often the better match at that height.

### Can I order a custom width or length?

Yes, and it costs nothing. Width, length, and colors outside our standard library are all part of a made-to-order commission. Every tie is cut after your order is placed, so there is no stock size to work around.

### What is the tie’s head and the tie’s end?

The head is the narrow tip that finishes behind the blade. The end is the wide tip that hangs in front, and it is the measurement people mean when they say a tie is 3 inches. The junction point is where the cut panels are joined.

### How big is the pocket square?

The pocket square in our tie and pocket square set is 17 inches square, finished with a hand-stitched edge, which gives enough body for a classic fold. A different size can be made at no extra cost.

### How should I care for a silk necktie?

Dry clean only. Untie the knot by reversing the steps rather than pulling the blade through, roll the tie loosely or hang it, and let it rest a day between wears so the bias cut can recover.
