## How to Take Your Own Measurements for a Perfect Fit

All you need is a flexible measuring tape, a mirror, and ideally a friend to help with the shoulder and back measurements. Wear close-fitting clothing so the tape sits against your body, stand naturally with your weight even on both feet, and keep the tape level and snug with room for one finger. Below is every measurement we may ask for. You do not have to get each number perfect: we send a free test garment before cutting your final cloth, so the fit is confirmed on your body first.

### Bust circumference

Stand straight with your arms relaxed and wrap the tape around the fullest part of your bust, keeping it level and snug with room for one finger.

### Natural waist

Measure around the narrowest part of your waist, usually just above the belly button, keeping the tape level with room for one finger.

### Waist (trouser or skirt)

Measure around the area where you normally wear your trousers or skirts, keeping the tape level with room for one finger.

### Hips and seat

Stand with your feet together and measure around the widest part of your hips and seat, keeping the tape level.

5

### Thigh circumference

Measure around the fullest part of your upper thigh, keeping the tape snug with room for one finger.

6

### Front rise

Sit down and measure from your natural waist at the front, down between your legs to the seat, to capture the rise for trousers.

7

### Inseam

Measure from the highest point of the crotch down the inside of your leg to the same hem point used for the trouser length measurement.

8

### Outseam and trouser length

Measure from the top of your waistband down the outside of your leg to the point where you want the trouser hem to finish.

9

### Shoulder width

Measure across your back from the edge of one shoulder to the edge of the other, keeping the tape flat.

10

### Sleeve or arm length

Measure from the edge of your shoulder, along the outside of your relaxed arm, to your desired sleeve length.

11

### Bicep (upper arm)

With your muscles relaxed, measure around the fullest part of your upper arm, keeping the tape snug with room for one finger.

12

### Neck circumference

Measure around the base of your neck where a collar would sit, keeping the tape level with room for one finger.

13

### Jacket or back length

Measure from the base of your neck down the center back to where you want the jacket to end.

14

### Dress or skirt length

Measure from your natural waist for a skirt, or from the shoulder for a dress, down to your desired hem.

Every garment is made to your measurements with no standard sizing, and a free test garment confirms the fit on your body before we cut your final cloth. Bust, waist, hips, thighs, rise, inseam, shoulders, sleeve, and length, all cut to you.

## Frequently asked questions about measuring yourself

### How do I measure my bust, waist and length for a custom garment?

Use a flexible tape. For the bust, wrap it around the fullest part with the tape level. For the waist, measure the narrowest part, usually just above the belly button. For length, measure from the shoulder or natural waist down to your desired hem. Keep the tape level and snug with room for one finger, and measure over close-fitting clothing rather than bulky layers.

### How do I measure my waist, hips, thighs and rise?

Measure the waist at the narrowest point, the hips around the widest part of your hips and seat, and the thigh around the fullest part of the upper leg. For the rise, sit down and measure from the front of your natural waist, down between your legs to the seat. Keep the tape level and snug throughout.

### Where can I get my measurements taken?

Any local tailor or seamstress can take your measurements in a few minutes, and many fabric or bridal shops offer measuring too. You can also measure yourself at home with a flexible tape using this guide, or have a friend help with the back and shoulder measurements. Because we send a free test garment before cutting your final cloth, the fit is confirmed on your body regardless of who takes the first measurements.

### What do I need to measure myself?

Just a flexible measuring tape, a mirror, and ideally a friend to help with the shoulder and back measurements. Wear close-fitting clothing so the tape sits against your body, and stand naturally with your weight even on both feet.

### How tight should the measuring tape be?

Snug but not tight. The tape should sit flat against your body with just enough room to slip one finger underneath. Pulling it too tight gives measurements that are too small, and leaving it loose makes the garment baggy.

### What if my measurements are not perfect?

That is exactly why we send a free test garment. Give us your best measurements, and we build a test version in a stand-in cloth to your numbers and ship it to you. You try it on, we adjust from there, and nothing is cut in your final fabric until the fit is confirmed.
