## Questions & Answers

**Q:** Why Melton wool and not leather or polyester like other sellers?  
**A:** The original screen-used garment from the iconic 2008 film was crafted by the wardrobe department using wool, not leather or cheap synthetics. Heavyweight Melton wool is a dense, tightly woven luxury textile with a smooth, matte surface that lacks any synthetic gloss. It drapes with dramatic physical weight, holds structural sharp lines perfectly, and never looks like a party costume under flash photography or convention lights. Low-grade polyester alternatives reflect light poorly, revealing their low quality instantly. Leather alternatives completely distort the historical silhouette and are much less breathable for extended wear. Real winter wool is the only screen-accurate material choice.

**Q:** Why does the coat feature three vents in the back construction?  
**A:** The intricate triple rear panel vent configuration is an exact, screen-accurate detail modeled from the original film pattern. Beyond its archival value, it serves an important functional purpose: a three-vent layout allows the heavy fabric drop to open fluidly when you are walking, giving the long overcoat its signature swirling silhouette in motion. Standard center slits or closed panels cause the lower coat body to hang completely stiffly from your hips, which restricts your movement and looks wrong in photographs. Most generic costume makers skip this complex step to save on labor, but we build it precisely to match the film's layout.

**Q:** What is a canvas front interior and why is it mandatory for this coat?  
**A:** An authentic canvas front is a traditional tailoring layer composed of hand-woven horsehair canvas that is carefully basted inside the coat chest panel, sitting completely unattached between the outer Melton wool and the inner lining. Mass-produced costume garments rely on cheap chemical glues to fuse their inner layers together, which rapidly degrades, puckers, and creates ugly permanent bubbles after a single round of dry cleaning. A floating canvas front naturally breaks in and conforms to your body proportions over time, keeping your wide peak lapels rolling beautifully and ensuring the jacket front retains its structural integrity across decades of use.
