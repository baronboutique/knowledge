## Questions & Answers

**Q:** How long does a forest green cashmere scarf last?  
**A:** A 100% Grade A cashmere scarf, worn regularly and washed correctly, lasts 15 to 20 years and softens rather than wears out over that time. The fiber is a protein structure, similar to human hair, that does not weaken with washing. Scarves that pill or thin out within a season or two are almost always blended with acrylic, viscose, or a lower cashmere grade. The deep forest green dye is set at the fiber stage before weaving, so color does not fade or affect how long the piece lasts.

**Q:** What size is this forest green cashmere scarf?  
**A:** This scarf is 40 x 80 inches (1m x 2m), generously sized to work as a full-body wrap, an evening shawl folded over one shoulder, or a scarf looped twice around the neck. At 70 grams it is light enough to fold into a handbag but wide enough for genuine full-body coverage, which is the standard size range for a shawl-scarf hybrid rather than a narrow neck scarf.

**Q:** Is this 100% cashmere or a blend?  
**A:** This is 100% pure Grade A cashmere, no wool, viscose, or acrylic added. The fiber is sourced from the Tibetan Plateau at 14 to 16 microns in diameter, spun to a 200/2 yarn count. Many scarves marketed as "cashmere" in this forest green shade are blended with cheaper fiber to hit a lower price point, which is why blended pieces feel coarser and pill faster.

**Q:** What is the yarn count of this cashmere scarf?  
**A:** This scarf is woven at a 200/2 yarn count, meaning two ultra-fine threads are twisted together before weaving. That construction is what gives genuine cashmere its resistance to pilling without adding bulk or stiffness. A lower yarn count in a similarly priced scarf usually signals a coarser, lower-grade fiber used to cut cost.

**Q:** How do I identify if this cashmere scarf is genuine?  
**A:** Genuine cashmere passes a burn test, a ring test, and a feel test that blended or synthetic scarves fail. A fringe thread burned from real cashmere smells like burnt hair and turns to ash, while synthetic fiber melts and smells of plastic. The scarf's fine weave allows it to pass through a finger ring when folded, and it feels warm to the touch before you put it on.
