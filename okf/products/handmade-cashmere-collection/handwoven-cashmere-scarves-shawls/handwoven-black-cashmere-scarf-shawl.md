The Cashmere

## What Makes This Cashmere Soft, Light and Warm

Warmth in cashmere comes from fineness, not weight. Very fine fibers trap still air close to your skin, which is how a 70 gram scarf can feel warm around your neck.

| 14 to 16 microns | Under US labeling rules, cashmere must average 19 microns or finer. This fiber is finer still, so it feels soft rather than prickly. |
| --- | --- |
| 35 to 55 mm staple | Longer fibers hold in the yarn better, so the scarf pills and sheds less as it ages. |
| 200/2 yarn | Two very fine threads twisted together give strength while keeping the scarf soft enough to knot without bulk. |
| About 70 g | Folds small enough for a handbag when you do not need it. |

Compared with a wool shawl draped over shoulders, or a wool scarf wrapped around neck on a cold day, cashmere is finer and softer, so it does not itch. A black wool scarf for women usually has to be thicker and heavier to give the same warmth. Next to a black silk scarf, elegant but cool, or a black velvet scarf, heavy and stiff, cashmere drapes softly and stays warm. Shopping for a cashmere pashmina scarf draped like this? Pashmina is the traditional name for very fine cashmere, but shops today often use it for blends, so check the label: this one is 100% cashmere. To test any scarf at home, see our **[guide to telling real cashmere from a blend](https://baronboutique.com/aquamarine-blue-handwoven-cashmere-scarf-shawl/#bb-real)**.

Care

## Caring for a Black Cashmere Scarf

A deep color can release a little dye in its first wash, so wash it on its own the first time. Hand wash in cold or lukewarm water with a mild detergent or wool wash, soak for 8 to 12 minutes, press the water through without wringing, rinse in cool water and dry it flat on a towel out of direct sun.

Black shows lint and loose fibers more than other colors. A soft clothes brush or a lint roller used lightly takes them off. For storage, fold it clean with cedar or lavender.

The open weave can catch on rings, bracelets, zips and bag straps. If a thread pulls, ease it back from the other side with a blunt needle. Never cut it.
