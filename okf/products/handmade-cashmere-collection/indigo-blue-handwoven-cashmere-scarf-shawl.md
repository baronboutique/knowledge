The Cashmere

## What Makes This Cashmere Soft, Light and Warm

Warmth in cashmere comes from fineness, not weight. Very fine fibers trap still air close to your skin, which is how a 70 gram scarf can feel warm around your neck.

| 14 to 16 microns | Under US labeling rules, cashmere must average 19 microns or finer. This fiber is finer still, so it feels soft rather than prickly. |
| --- | --- |
| 35 to 55 mm staple | Longer fibers hold in the yarn better, so the scarf pills and sheds less as it ages. |
| 200/2 yarn | Two very fine threads twisted together give strength while keeping the scarf soft enough to knot without bulk. |
| About 70 g | Folds small enough for a handbag when you do not need it. |

Searching for a cashmere pashmina scarf draped like this? Pashmina is the traditional name for very fine cashmere, but shops today often use it for blends, so check the label: this one is 100% cashmere. Compared with a blue silk scarf, which is elegant but cool, cashmere is warmer and softer at the neck. To test any scarf at home, see our **[guide to telling real cashmere from a blend](https:///aquamarine-blue-handwoven-cashmere-scarf-shawl/#bb-real)**.

Care

## Caring for a Deep Blue Cashmere Scarf

A deep color can release a little dye in its first wash, so wash it on its own the first time. Hand wash in cold or lukewarm water with a mild detergent or wool wash, soak for 8 to 12 minutes, press the water through without wringing, rinse in cool water and dry it flat on a towel out of direct sun. A steamer or low iron removes creases.

Between wears, hang it somewhere airy. For storage, fold it clean with cedar or lavender.

The open weave can catch on rings, bracelets, zips and bag straps. If a thread pulls, ease it back from the other side with a blunt needle. Never cut it.
