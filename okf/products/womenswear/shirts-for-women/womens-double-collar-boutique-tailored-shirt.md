## A Free Test Shirt to Place the Darts

Your shirt is first made in cotton from your measurements and sent to you free; the test shirt is yours to keep. It is how we place the darts on your body rather than on a size chart.

Darts only work when they point at the right spot. A front dart that ends too high pulls under the bust; one that ends too low leaves a pocket of loose cloth. On the test shirt we check where each dart starts and stops, how much it takes in at the waist, and whether the collar sits evenly around your neck. Stand side-on to a mirror and look for diagonal pulls; those tell us which dart to move.

Shirt Darts

## What Are Darts on a Women’s Shirt?

What are darts on a woman’s shirt? A dart is a narrow, tapered fold of cloth stitched down on the inside, wide at one end and finishing in a point. It takes out cloth where the body narrows, so a flat piece of fabric curves around the bust, the waist and the back. Darts in women’s shirts do the job that a looser cut leaves undone.

| Front waist darts | Run up from the waist toward the bust; they shape the front and stop the shirt billowing when tucked |
| --- | --- |
| Back waist darts | Run up the back on either side; they take in the hollow of the lower back |
| Bust darts | Start at the side seam and point to the bust; used on some styles for a fuller bust |
| Double-pointed darts | Pointed at both ends, widest at the waist; the shape of the long darts on this shirt |

A women’s shirt with darts sits close without being tight, because the cloth is removed only where your body is narrower. A shirt without them relies on stretch or extra width, which is why a boxy shirt looks bigger than the woman wearing it. Dress shirts with darts also tuck better: there is less cloth to bunch at the waistband. A women’s blouse with darts works the same way; a blouse with darts in a soft cloth drapes, while a tailored blouse in Oxford holds its line.

From behind you can see the two back darts. On a darted shirt, women often notice the back first: it is the part that looks untidy on a ready-made shirt and neat on a fitted one. If you prefer a softer line, ask for shallower darts; the test shirt shows the difference.

## Questions About the Double Collar Shirt

### What are darts on a women’s shirt?

Darts are tapered folds stitched on the inside of the shirt that remove cloth where the body narrows. They shape the shirt to the bust, waist and back so it fits close without pulling.

### Why do women’s shirts have darts?

Because a woman’s shape changes more between bust and waist than a straight piece of cloth allows. Darts take out the difference, so the shirt follows the body instead of billowing.

### Can I have shallower darts or none at all?

Yes. Tell us on the measurement form how close you want the fit; we can make the darts shallower, and the free test shirt shows the effect before the Oxford is cut.

### What is a double collar shirt?

A shirt with two collars layered in one neckline, so both edges show. On this shirt the top collar buttons down at the points, which keeps the layers flat and crisp.

### Can I wear a double collar shirt with a jacket?

Yes. The second layer gives the collar extra body, so it sits neatly against a lapel; keep the top button open.

### Is Oxford cotton good for a work shirt?

Yes, for most offices. It is a little more textured and casual than poplin, holds a collar well, has a natural stretch and irons easily.

### How do I care for it?

Wash it cool with like colors, or dry clean it for a longer life. Press both collars from the points toward the neck so the layers stay flat.

### Is the test shirt free, and how long does it take?

Yes, the test shirt is free and yours to keep. It takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch; once you confirm the fit, your final shirt takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch. Order as early as you can.
