## Placing the Waist and the Flounce

Two corrections come up most often on this dress. The first is the waist: if it sits even an inch too low, the tie belt slides down and the bodice looks long. The second is the flounce line, which we raise or lower so the ruffle starts just below your knee rather than at a point set for someone else’s height.

Gingham also shows every fault in the cut, because the checks have to run straight across the bodice. The test dress lets us confirm the bust and the back V in cotton first.

## How a Ruffle Hem Midi Dress Should Fall

| Where the flounce starts | Just below the knee, so the straight part of the skirt covers the knee and the movement begins below it. |
| --- | --- |
| Where it ends | At the narrowest part of the calf for a classic midi, or lower for a longer line; never at the widest part of the calf. |
| Depth of the flounce | Scaled to your height, so the ruffle stays a detail rather than half the skirt. |
| Vent-less hem | The flounce gives walking room, so there is no slit to break the check. |

A midi dress with a ruffle hem is a matter of proportion more than decoration. The flounce adds width at the bottom of the dress, which balances the hips and makes the waist look narrower, but only when it sits at the right height. That is why we cut the line from your measurements instead of from a size.

Cotton Poplin

## Why Cotton Poplin

Poplin is a tight plain weave of fine cotton yarns, the same weave used for crisp dress shirts. It is light enough for a hot day and firm enough to hold the curve of the flounce, where a softer cotton would hang flat. Being all cotton, it breathes and can be washed at home.

| Unlined | Cooler in summer heat; a skin-tone slip helps in very bright sun on light grounds. |
| --- | --- |
| No stretch | The fit comes from the cut, which is why the test dress comes first. |
| Creases | Cotton creases more than a blend; it presses out quickly while slightly damp. |
| Care | Machine wash cold on a gentle cycle and hang to dry. |

Questions & Answers

## Questions About the Gingham Ruffle Hem Midi Dress

### Where should the ruffle hem of a midi dress fall?

The flounce should start just below the knee and end at the narrowest part of the calf, or a little above the ankle bone for a longer midi. If it starts too high it shortens the leg; if it ends at the widest part of the calf it widens it. We set both points from your height and leg length, and you can enter any length you want on the measurement form.

### Is gingham still in style?

Gingham returns every warm season because it is a woven check rather than a printed trend, and it has been worn for summer dresses for generations. What makes a gingham dress look dated is a loose, shapeless fit, not the check. Cut to your measurements with a clean neckline, it reads as classic.

### Is gingham a flattering pattern?

Yes, if the check size suits you. A small check, like our True gingham, reads almost as a solid color from a few steps away and is the quieter choice; a larger check, like Midnight, is bolder and draws more attention. On this dress the check also bends through the flounce, which shows the movement of the hem.

### What is the difference between 100% cotton and cotton poplin?

Cotton is the fiber and poplin is the weave. Poplin is a tight plain weave with fine yarns, which gives a smooth, crisp surface. This dress is both: 100% cotton, woven as poplin.

### Is poplin a cheap fabric?

Not by nature. Poplin is the same weave used for good dress shirts; its quality depends on the fineness of the yarn and how closely it is woven. A dense, all-cotton poplin like this one holds a crisp line and softens with washing instead of thinning out.

### What are the downsides of cotton poplin?

It creases more than a blended fabric and has no stretch, so the fit has to come from the cut. Light grounds can show through in strong sunlight because the dress is unlined; if that concerns you, a skin-tone slip solves it. In return, it breathes well in heat and can go in the washing machine.

### Why cotton poplin for a summer dress?

Poplin is light and breathable, yet crisp enough to hold the shape of a ruffle without going limp, which softer cottons such as voile cannot do. It also takes repeated washing, which matters for a dress you will wear all summer.

### Can I machine wash it?

Yes. Wash it on a gentle cold cycle, then hang it to dry and press it while slightly damp. The flounce irons more easily if you work from the inside of the hem.

### Can I wear it without the belt?

Yes, the tie belt is removable. Tied, it marks the waist; without it, the dress falls more softly from the bodice. We place the waist on the test dress so it sits correctly either way.

### Can I add sleeves or pockets?

Yes. Add the request to the comment box of the measurement form as you order, or send it by email with your order number. Short sleeves, cap sleeves and side seam pockets can all be added to this pattern.

### Can I see the gingham before I order?

Yes. Our **[swatch service](https://baronboutique.com/swatch-service/)** sends up to four samples, each about 2 by 4 inches, usually within 4 to 6 business days. Quote product number 3200006 and the colors you want to compare; the courier fee is credited toward your first order.

### How long will my dress take?

The cotton test dress takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch. Once you approve the fit, your gingham dress takes another 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch, so order well ahead of the date you need it.
