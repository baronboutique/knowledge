## A Black Sheath Dress With White Piping, Cut So Every Line Sits Straight

Contrast piping is the least forgiving detail a dress can have. A white line on black cloth traces every seam, so if the neckline gapes or the hem tilts, the piping draws a diagram of the fault for everyone to see. That is why piped dresses look sharp on the hanger and so often disappointing on the body.

## Getting the Lines Right First

On a piped dress the test dress checks three things more closely than usual. The V must lie flat against the chest, because any gap shows as a wobbling white line. The armholes must sit close, because piping at a gaping armhole looks like a loose thread. And the hem must hang level, because a straight line of piping makes the smallest tilt obvious.

Where the right fit would change the look in the photos, such as a shorter V or a lower collar, we explain the trade-off and you choose.

**Choose the cloth and piping**  
 Pick the dress color and lining in the selector, enter your measurements, and write the piping color in the comment box if you want something other than white.

2

**A cotton version comes first**  
 Your test dress is cut in cotton to your measurements, with the collar and V in place; it takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch.

3

**Check the lines the piping will follow**  
 Look at the V and the collar in a mirror: the V should lie flat and the collar should stand close without pressing. Raise your arms; the armholes should not gape. Check that the hem runs level all the way round.

4

**The wool is cut and piped**  
 We correct the lines, cut the wool and set the piping; the final dress takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch. The test dress is yours to keep.

## Questions About the Piped Sheath Dress

### Can I choose a different piping color?

Yes. Choose the dress color in the selector and write the piping color you want in the comment box of the measurement form, or email us with your order number. Navy with white piping, white with navy, or black with red are all possible; a strong contrast shows the lines most clearly.

### What is piping on a dress?

Piping is a narrow, rounded trim: a strip of fabric cut on the bias, folded around a fine cord and sewn into a seam or along an edge. It outlines the shape of the dress, so on this one it draws the V, the collar, the armholes and the hem in a clean contrasting line.

### What is the difference between piping and binding?

Binding wraps around a raw edge and shows on both sides as a flat band. Piping is set into a seam and shows only as a raised line on the outside. Piping looks crisper and more tailored, which is why it suits a structured dress like this.

### Will the piping pucker around the neckline?

Not when it is cut on the bias and eased around the curves, which is how we set it. Puckered piping usually comes from a strip cut on the straight grain or a neckline that does not fit, so it is pulled out of shape. We check the neckline on the test dress before the piping goes in.

### Is this a sheath or an A-line dress?

Both, in a way. It follows the body like a sheath through the bust and waist, then eases out slightly below the hip, so the hem has a little more room than a straight sheath. It looks tailored but is easier to walk and sit in.

### Will it suit me if I am fuller at the hips or stomach?

Yes. A close sheath can outline the stomach and pull across fuller hips; the slight flare below the hip on this cut gives that area room, and the vertical piping at the front draws the eye up to the neckline. The test dress shows us exactly how much ease you need.

### Can I wear it to work?

Yes. A dark sheath with a stand collar is formal enough for most offices on its own, and it works under a short jacket. The piping adds detail without needing jewelry.

### Is the stand collar comfortable?

It is fitted to the base of your neck on the test dress so it stands close without pressing. If you prefer a lower collar, tell us and we lower it before the wool is cut.

### Can I see the cloth before I order?

Yes. Our **[swatch service](https://baronboutique.com/swatch-service/)** sends up to four samples, each about 2 by 4 inches, usually within 4 to 6 business days. Quote product number 3200010 and the colors you want to compare; the courier fee is credited toward your first order.

### How long will my dress take?

The cotton test dress takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch. Once you approve the fit, your wool dress takes another 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch, so plan around the date you need it.
