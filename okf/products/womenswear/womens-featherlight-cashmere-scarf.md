## Questions & Answers

**Q:** How can a full-size cashmere scarf weigh only 70 grams?  
**A:** Three specifications, working together. The fiber measures 14 to 16 microns, fine enough to spin very thin. The yarn is spun to a 200/2 count — two ultra-fine singles twisted together, where the twist supplies the strength a thread that fine would otherwise lack. And the weave is deliberately open, a gossamer structure with air between the threads rather than a dense cloth. The result is roughly 34 grams per square meter across 40 by 80 inches. For comparison, cotton t-shirt jersey usually runs 150 to 180 grams per square meter.

**Q:** How small does it fold down?  
**A:** Small enough to sit flat in one open palm, and small enough to slip into a trouser or coat pocket rather than needing a bag. That is the whole 40 by 80 inch scarf folded, not a corner of it. The photographs above are not staged with a smaller sample — a fine open weave at 70 grams simply compresses to almost nothing, which is why people carry this piece when they would leave a heavier wrap at home.

**Q:** Is a scarf this light actually warm?  
**A:** Warmer than its weight suggests, but it is not a winter scarf and we would not sell it as one. Cashmere insulates by trapping air rather than by mass, and an open weave traps a surprising amount. In practice this piece is right for cool rather than cold: flights, air-conditioned offices, evenings in spring and autumn, a summer wedding. For genuine winter cold, our heavier handwoven shawls or the hand-felted wraps will serve you better, and we would rather point you there than have you disappointed.

**Q:** What does a 200/2 yarn count mean?  
**A:** The first number describes how fine the spun yarn is — the higher it is, the thinner the thread. The second number is how many of those threads are twisted together. So 200/2 is two very fine singles plied into one yarn. The twist matters: a single unplied thread at this fineness would break under loom tension, which is why very light cashmere is either plied like this or spun coarser to survive the process. It is also why the finished cloth resists pulling apart despite weighing almost nothing.

**Q:** Will a cloth this fine pill or snag?  
**A:** Less than heavier cashmere, which surprises people. Pilling is caused by short fibers working loose from the yarn and tangling — it is a function of staple length, not of weight. These fibers run 35 to 55mm, long enough to stay anchored. What a fine open weave is more vulnerable to is snagging on rough jewelry, watch clasps or handbag hardware, so take reasonable care around those. Light surface pilling in the first few wears is loose fiber shedding and stops; remove it with a cashmere comb, never a razor.

**Q:** Can you hand wash a featherlight cashmere scarf, or is it dry-clean only?  
**A:** Hand wash it — it is not dry-clean only, and hand washing is the better method. Use cool to lukewarm water with a wool or cashmere shampoo, do not wring, press the water out in a towel and dry flat in shade. Cashmere genuinely improves with washing as the fibers bloom and the handle softens. Dry cleaning solvents strip the natural lanolin that keeps cashmere supple, which is why frequently dry-cleaned pieces begin to feel papery over time.

**Q:** Is it big enough to use as a shawl or a travel blanket?  
**A:** Yes to both. At 40 by 80 inches it is full shawl width, worn open across the shoulders or wrapped across the body, and it is long enough to use as a light blanket on a flight or for a nap. That combination — full coverage at 70 grams — is the reason people carry it when they would not carry a heavier wrap. It folds small enough to sit in a coat pocket or a handbag without taking up meaningful space.

**Q:** Can it be monogrammed?  
**A:** Yes, and a plain-woven solid piece is the easiest kind to embroider well, because there is no pattern for the thread to compete with. We embroider single pieces as well as sets — initials, a name, a date, or your own logo. Email the initials or your artwork to contact@baronboutique.com and we will come back with a quote and a stitch proof before anything is sewn. Note that a lighter thread is used on cloth this fine, so the lettering sits flat rather than proud of the surface.
