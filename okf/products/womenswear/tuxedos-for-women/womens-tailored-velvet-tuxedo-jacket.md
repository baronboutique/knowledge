## Velvet Cannot Be Let Out, So the Fit Comes First

With wool, a tailor can let a seam out or take it in and press the old line away. Velvet does not allow that: the old stitching leaves holes and a crushed line in the pile that stay visible. So the corrections are made on a cotton jacket instead, before the velvet is touched.

The corrections we make most often on women’s jackets are at the bust and the waist. When the bust does not follow the rest of the measurements, a single-button jacket either strains at the button or opens above it, so we may move the button or add shaping, keeping the button close enough that the front never gapes. If a change would alter the style, such as an extra dart through the velvet front, we explain it and you choose between an exact fit and the exact look in the photos.

## What Makes a Velvet Tuxedo Jacket Different

| The pile has a direction | Velvet looks darker from one side and lighter from the other. Every panel is cut with the pile running the same way, otherwise the sleeves and the body look like two shades. |
| --- | --- |
| It is pressed by steam, not by iron | A hot iron flattens the pile and leaves a shine, so seams are opened with steam and a light touch. |
| Cotton and polyester blend | The cotton gives the deep, soft color; the polyester helps the pile recover from sitting and keeps the jacket from crushing as easily as pure cotton velvet. |
| Silk satin, not polyester satin | Silk satin on the lapels has a softer, deeper sheen than the glassy look of synthetic satin, and it is the classic finish for a tuxedo. |

Cheaper velvet blazers usually fail in two places: the pile wears bald at the cuffs and elbows, and the front loses its shape because nothing inside holds it. A canvas front and a dense pile are what keep this jacket looking right after many evenings out.

## Questions About the Women’s Velvet Tuxedo Jacket

### What do I wear with a velvet tuxedo jacket?

For formal evenings, black tailored trousers, an ivory silk blouse and heels. For dinner, a fine knit and dark jeans let the satin lapels do the work. Jewel-toned velvet such as wine or green looks rich against black.

### What is the velvet made of?

A blend of cotton and polyester. The cotton gives the soft, deep color, and the polyester helps the pile resist crushing.

### Are the lapels real silk?

Yes. The peak lapels and the collar are faced in silk satin.

### Can I wear it to a wedding?

Yes, especially in the colder months and in the evening. As a guest, choose a rich color with black trousers; as a bride, ask about white or cream options through the Ask a question about this piece form.

### Is it warm?

Yes. Velvet is warmer than a wool tuxedo blazer, so it suits fall and winter evenings most.

### Will velvet crush when I sit?

All velvet marks a little with pressure. Hang the jacket after wearing and the pile lifts back; steam from inside if a mark stays.

### Why is a test jacket so important for velvet?

Velvet cannot be taken in or let out without leaving visible marks. That is why every fit correction is made in cotton first.

### Can I change the lapel, buttons or pockets?

Yes. Spell out the change in the comment box of the measurement form, or email us with your order number. A shawl collar, satin-covered buttons, jetted pockets without flaps or a second button can all be made.

### I have no tailor nearby. How do I measure?

A helper makes it easier. Alone, measure your bust, natural waist and hips, then a jacket that fits you well; we can guide you by video call or WhatsApp.

### Can I see the velvet before ordering?

Yes. Our **[swatch service](https://baronboutique.com/swatch-service/)** sends up to four samples of about 2 by 4 inches. They usually arrive within 4 to 6 business days. Quote product number 2800004 and the colors you want to compare; the courier fee is credited toward your first order.

### How long will the velvet jacket take?

The cotton test jacket takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch. The velvet jacket then takes 3 to 4 weeks and arrives 5 to 10 days after it ships.

### How do I care for velvet?

Dry clean only and rarely. Brush lightly in the direction of the pile, store on a shaped hanger with space around it, and never iron the velvet.
