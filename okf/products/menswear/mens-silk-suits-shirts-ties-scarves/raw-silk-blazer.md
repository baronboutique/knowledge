Silk You Can See, Silk You Can Feel

## Most Silk Blend Jackets Put the Silk Where You Look and the Polyester Where You Live

Search for a silk jacket and most of what comes back is a blend. Silk blend blazers are typically woven at somewhere between 20 and 55 percent silk, with polyester or viscose making up the rest, and then lined with a synthetic that has no silk in it at all. So the small amount of silk you paid for sits on the outside, where you only ever look at it, and the surface actually touching your back and arms for eight hours is plastic. That is the part that makes a jacket clammy by the afternoon, and it is the part nobody checks before buying.

This blazer is built the other way around. The shell is 100% raw silk with nothing blended into it, and the lining can be upgraded to genuine satin silk rather than acetate. Raw silk is silk that has not been degummed, so the yarn keeps its natural sericin and the cloth keeps the slub and grain that come with it. You can see the texture across a room and feel it under your hand, which is the entire reason to buy silk rather than a fabric that photographs like silk.

The result wears like a sport coat, not eveningwear. There is no polish to catch a room’s light, so a 100% raw silk blazer does not announce itself the way a satin or dupioni jacket does. It goes over a shirt or a fine knit, it is cut to your measurements on a hand-basted canvas front with hand-set armholes, and before we cut your silk we send a free test blazer so the fit is confirmed at home first.

## A Textured Cloth Does Not Forgive a Loose Fit, It Advertises It

There is a reasonable assumption that an irregular fabric hides an irregular fit. It does the opposite. The slub and grain in raw silk are meant to read as the character of the cloth, and the moment the chest pulls or the shoulder sits forward, those same irregularities stop looking deliberate and start looking like a jacket that belongs to somebody else. Reading the Cloth

## Fourteen Raw Silks, and They Do Not All Behave the Same Way

Raw silk does not read identically in every color, and it is worth knowing that before you choose. In the neutrals, Natural, Grey, Steel Grey, Brown and Unprocessed, the raw character is at its most visible: a matte, linen-like grain with almost no reflective sheen, closer to a handwoven cloth than to eveningwear silk. The Unprocessed colorway, undyed and left in its natural flecked state, shows this most clearly and is the cloth photographed on this page.

In the saturated colors, Hot Pink, Blood Red, Petrol Blue, Aqua Green, Gold, Yellow, Orange and Red, the retained sericin still catches light, but unevenly, in streaks that follow the slub rather than across a flat plane. That broken light-catch is what keeps a strong color from reading as costume. A processed silk in the same shade reflects evenly and can look theatrical. Raw silk gives the color depth instead of glare.

A black raw silk blazer and a navy raw silk blazer sit in a category of their own. In those two shades the texture does the work and the color stays quiet, so the jacket reads as a well-cut sport coat first and as silk second. If you want the fabric without the announcement, those are the two to ask about.

## FAQ

### Is this a silk blend blazer or 100% silk?

This is 100% raw silk with nothing blended into it. Most silk blend blazers sold today are woven at 20 to 55 percent silk, with polyester or viscose making up the remainder, because blending lowers the cost and makes the cloth easier to cut on a production line. A silk blend mens blazer at 30 percent silk has the price of a compromise and the hand of one. The difference is immediate once you have both in your hands.

### What is raw silk, and how is it different from dupioni?

Raw silk is silk that has not been degummed, so the yarn still contains sericin, the natural protein that binds the cocoon fiber together. That retained sericin gives raw silk a stiffer, slightly grainy hand and a low, uneven light-catch. Dupioni is a processed silk woven from paired-cocoon threads into a crisp weave with a pronounced, even sheen, which is why it is the silk most people associate with formal and bridal wear. Both are genuine silk. The difference is what happens to the yarn before it is woven, and that determines whether the finished cloth shines or textures.

### Can the blazer be lined in real silk instead of acetate?

Yes. We can line your 100% raw silk blazer in genuine satin silk, the same cloth we use for our satin silk shirts, so the surface against your skin is silk rather than a synthetic. It moves over a shirt without dragging and holds temperature better across a long evening. Satin silk lining is a custom upgrade above the listed price, so tell us the color you want when you enquire and we will quote it by email before you commit.

### Do you make a black raw silk blazer or a navy raw silk blazer?

Yes, black and navy raw silk are available on request. In those two shades the texture carries the jacket and the color stays quiet, so it reads as a well-cut sport coat first and as silk second. If you want a silk jacket that does not announce itself as one, black and navy are the two to ask about. Contact us for current availability on those cloths.

### Is a raw silk jacket the same thing as a silk sport coat?

In practice, yes. Sport coat, blazer and jacket are used interchangeably for this garment, and a raw silk jacket sits closer to the sport coat end of the range because the matte, textured cloth carries no built-in formality. It is not eveningwear and it does not read as a suit jacket separated from its trousers. It works over a shirt or a fine knit, which is what a sport coat is meant to do.

### How long does a raw silk blazer last with regular wear?

A raw silk blazer built on a canvas front and made to measure will hold its shape and texture for 15 to 20 years with proper dry-cleaning. The limiting factor is almost always the front construction: fused interlinings delaminate after repeated cleaning and cause the chest to bubble, while a hand-basted canvas front holds indefinitely. Raw silk itself does not lose its slub or grain with age the way a polished cloth can lose its sheen, so the character that makes the jacket distinctive on day one is still there years later.

### Which measurement matters most for a raw silk blazer?

The chest is the most critical, because raw silk’s stiffer hand shows an imprecise fit more readily than a soft or draping cloth would. A chest that runs even slightly wide creates visible pulling across the slub rather than smoothing out the way a fluid fabric might. You enter all measurements on the product page at time of order, and before we cut the silk we send a cotton test blazer built to those numbers so the fit is confirmed on your body first.

### Is this made to my measurements or does it come in standard sizes?

Every blazer is made entirely to your measurements. There are no standard sizes and no pre-made stock. The pattern is cut from your numbers, and a free test blazer confirms the fit at home before the raw silk is cut, which matters more for this cloth than most because its texture makes an imprecise fit harder to disguise.

### Can I wear a raw silk blazer to a wedding?

Yes, and it suits a daytime or outdoor wedding better than a polished silk does. The matte texture reads as considered rather than formal, so it works for a garden, coastal or summer ceremony where a lustrous jacket would look like eveningwear worn at the wrong hour. For a black-tie evening wedding a tuxedo is still the correct garment. For everything short of that, a 100% raw silk blazer in a neutral or a deep shade sits comfortably within the dress code.
