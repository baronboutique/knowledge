## Free Test Suit – Why We Do It Before Every Order

Super 160s is not a forgiving fabric. The finer the wool, the more precisely a jacket needs to be cut for the cloth to drape correctly. A chest that pulls slightly, a shoulder that sits a fraction too wide – on a heavier fabric these problems are less visible. On Super 160s they are not. This is why we will not cut your VBC cloth until the fit has been confirmed on a test garment.

Every Baron order begins with a complete test suit in cotton, built to your measurements and shipped to you by trackable courier. You try it on at home. You tell us what needs adjusting. We fix the pattern, then cut your final suit in the wool. The test suit is yours to keep. This is the only way to guarantee fit for a customer who cannot come to the workshop in person – and it is the standard we hold ourselves to for every order, regardless of where in the world it ships.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Vitale Barberis Canonico and why does it matter?

Vitale Barberis Canonico is an Italian wool mill based in Biella, a region of northern Italy that has been the center of European fine textile production for centuries. The mill has been operating since 1663 and is currently run by the thirteenth generation of the same family. VBC supplies fabric to many of the most respected tailors and suit houses in Europe. When a suit is described as using VBC cloth, it means the fabric was woven at that specific mill to their quality standards – it is a verifiable origin claim, not just a marketing label. Super 160s from VBC specifically refers to a fine-grade worsted wool with a luminous surface quality and a smooth, precise drape.

### What does Super 160s mean and how is it different from Super 120s or Super 100s?

The Super number refers to the fineness of the wool yarn. The higher the number, the finer the individual fibers, the smoother the fabric surface, and the more precise the drape. Super 100s is a good working wool – durable, reliable, used in quality ready-to-wear. Super 120s is finer, with a noticeably softer hand. Super 160s is finer still – the grade where the cloth has a luminous quality under light, where the surface feels smooth rather than textured, and where the drape is sharp and precise. The trade-off at higher grades is that finer wools require more careful handling and are less forgiving of poor fit – which is exactly why the free test suit process matters more, not less, at this quality level.

### What fit options are available?

Four silhouettes: slim (close to the body, contemporary), standard (classic tailored cut with comfortable room), relaxed (more volume throughout, easier movement), and athletic (built for broader shoulders and chest with a tapered waist). Because every suit is made to your measurements, you are not choosing a standard size – you are defining the amount of ease around your body. For a classic grey wool working suit, standard or slim are the most appropriate. Athletic is the right choice if you have a noticeably V-shaped upper body and have found that standard-cut jackets always pull across the back.

Yes. A grey or navy wool suit in Super 160s is appropriate for almost any wedding dress code from smart casual through to black tie optional. Medium grey is the most versatile wedding choice – it works as a guest at any dress code and as a groom at a less formal ceremony. Dark grey or navy photograph well under both natural and venue lighting. If the wedding has a strict black tie dress code, black is the correct choice. The fabric weight and quality are appropriate for a full wedding day – the suit will hold its shape and drape correctly from ceremony through to the last dance.
