## A Free Test Shirt in Cotton

Your first shirt is a test shirt in cotton, cut from your measurements and sent to you free. Keep it; it is yours. It does not always carry every style detail you choose, because its job is fit, not finish.

On dress shirts, three things change most often between the test shirt and the final shirt: the shirt length, the sleeve length and spare cloth at the back. So check those first. Tuck the shirt in and raise both arms; the tail should stay tucked. Let your arms hang; the cuff should reach the base of your thumb and show a little below a jacket sleeve. Then look at your back in a mirror; folds of loose cloth above the belt mean we can take the back in. Button the collar last and slide two fingers inside: snug, but not tight. Note anything you want changed, from collar spread to a French cuff, in your notes or in the comment box.

What Is Twill

## What Is a Twill Dress Shirt?

Shirting comes in four everyday weaves, and each one behaves differently.

| Twill, this shirt | Diagonal lines, soft sheen, drapes well and creases less; easy to iron |
| --- | --- |
| Poplin or broadcloth | Plain weave, smooth and crisp; the most formal look, but creases sooner and shows more through light colors |
| Oxford | Basket weave, textured and casual; see our **[pink Oxford shirt](https://baronboutique.com/mens-pink-oxford-cotton-shirt/)** |
| Herringbone | A twill that reverses direction to form a V; more texture still |

Twill covers everything from denim to chinos, which is why some people think of it as casual. Woven fine in cotton for shirting, it is a business staple. Next to poplin it is a little warmer, more forgiving of creases and less see-through in white or light blue, and the diagonal lines read as quiet texture rather than pattern. Some makers sell a twill Oxford, a mix of the two weaves; this is plain twill. For a smoother, finer cotton, see our **[Egyptian cotton dress shirt](https://baronboutique.com/mens-light-blue-egyptian-cotton-shirt/)**.

Our twill is 2-ply 80s. Two-ply means each thread is two fine yarns twisted together, which gives the cloth body and helps it last; 80s is the yarn count, a fine yarn that keeps the shirt smooth rather than coarse. It is a standard dress-shirt twill, not a heavy work-shirt cloth.

Questions & Answers

## Questions About the Men’s Twill Dress Shirt

### What is a twill dress shirt?

A dress shirt woven in twill, a weave whose threads step over at each row to form fine diagonal lines. The result is a cloth with a soft sheen and good drape that resists creasing better than plain poplin. It is a classic business shirt cloth.

### Twill or poplin: which is better for a dress shirt?

Poplin is smoother and crisper and looks the most formal; twill is softer, drapes better and creases less. For long days and travel, twill is the easier choice; for a sharp black-tie or boardroom look, poplin.

### Is twill a good fabric for a dress shirt?

Yes. Cotton twill is durable, slightly heavier than poplin and easy to iron, and comfortable through a long day. It suits office wear, business casual and wearing under tweed or flannel.

### Is a twill shirt formal enough for a suit?

Yes. A fine cotton twill in white, light blue or navy is fully appropriate under a suit and tie. Its texture reads as quiet depth rather than pattern.

### How should the back of a dress shirt fit?

The yoke should sit level across the shoulders, the side pleats should open only when you move, and the back should tuck in without billowing above the belt. If it pulls when you reach forward, the shoulders or back need more room.

### Can I choose French cuffs or a different collar?

Yes. The standard cuff is a convertible two-button cuff; French cuffs and other collar styles are available on request in the comment box on the measurement form or by email with your order number.

### Is it a non-iron shirt?

No. The cotton is plain and untreated, so it needs ironing after washing. Non-iron shirts are finished with a resin that saves the ironing but makes cotton feel stiffer and breathe less; untreated twill keeps the soft hand of cotton and irons easily while slightly damp.

### How do I wash a twill shirt?

Machine wash on a gentle cycle, hang to dry and iron while slightly damp. Dry cleaning now and then helps it last longer.

### What usually changes after the test shirt?

On dress shirts, the shirt length, the sleeve length and spare cloth at the back are what we adjust most often. The test shirt is where you tell us, so the final twill shirt is cut right the first time.

### Is the test shirt free, and how long does it take?

Yes, the test shirt is free and yours to keep. It takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch; once you confirm the fit, your final shirt takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch.
