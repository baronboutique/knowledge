## A Free Test Shirt, Then Your Oxford

Before your Oxford shirt is cut, we make it in cotton from your measurements and send it to you free. It is yours to keep. Its job is the fit, so it may not carry every style detail of the final shirt.

With French cuffs, sleeve length matters more than usual: the folded cuff should end at the wrist bone and show a little below a jacket sleeve, with the cufflink in view. On dress shirts we most often adjust three things after the test: the shirt length, the sleeve length and spare cloth at the back. So try the test shirt under a jacket, raise your arms to see that the tail stays tucked, and check the back in a mirror for loose folds above the belt.

Oxford or Dress Shirt

## Oxford Shirt or Dress Shirt? This One Is Both

An Oxford shirt and a dress shirt differ in two ways: the cloth and the cut. Oxford cloth is a basket weave, two fine yarns crossing one heavier yarn, which gives it a soft, slightly textured surface and a matte finish. A dress shirt usually comes in a smooth weave such as poplin or twill, with a collar made to hold a tie.

| Classic Oxford (button-down) | Basket-weave cloth, button-down collar, button cuffs, chest pocket; casual |
| --- | --- |
| This shirt | The same Oxford cloth, cut as a dress shirt: cutaway collar, French cuffs, no pocket; made for a suit and tie |
| Poplin or twill dress shirt | Smooth, finer weave with a slight sheen; the most formal choice; see our **[light blue twill dress shirt](https://baronboutique.com/mens-light-blue-twill-cotton-shirt/)** |

So can you wear an Oxford shirt with a suit? A button-down Oxford reads casual under tailoring, which is why it often looks out of place with a business suit. Cut like this, with a collar that spreads around a tie knot and cuffs that take links, Oxford cloth works for the office and for weddings while keeping its softer, warmer texture. For jeans and chinos, the button-down collar is the easier choice, and you can ask for it.

## Questions About the Men’s Pink Oxford Shirt

### Can you wear an Oxford shirt with a suit?

A classic button-down Oxford looks casual under a business suit. This one is cut as a dress shirt, with a cutaway collar for a tie and French cuffs for links, so it sits properly under a suit while keeping Oxford’s softer texture.

### What is the difference between an Oxford shirt and a dress shirt?

Oxford is a basket-weave cloth, softer and more textured than the smooth poplin or twill used for most dress shirts. Oxford shirts are usually casual button-downs; this one uses Oxford cloth with a dress-shirt collar and cuffs.

### Can I wear it to work?

Yes. With a tie and a suit it is office wear; without a tie, collar open under a blazer, it suits business casual.

### Can I get a button-down collar instead?

Yes. Write it in the comment box on the measurement page, or email us with your order number. Button cuffs instead of French cuffs can be requested the same way.

### Do I need cufflinks?

Yes, double French cuffs close with cufflinks. If you prefer buttons, ask for button cuffs when you order.

### Does Oxford cloth need ironing?

A little. The cotton is plain and untreated, not non-iron, so it breathes and stays soft; a light press after washing is enough, and the basket weave hides small creases.

### Is this the same as the women’s pink Oxford shirt?

It is the same family of cloth, but this shirt is cut for men, with a cutaway collar and French cuffs. For a shirt cut for women, see our women’s pink Oxford shirt.

### Is the test shirt free, and how long does it take?

Yes, the test shirt is free and yours to keep. It takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch; once you confirm the fit, your final shirt takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch.
