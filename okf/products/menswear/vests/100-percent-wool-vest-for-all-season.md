Free Test Waistcoat

## A Free Test Waistcoat in Cotton

Before the wool is cut, we make your waistcoat in cotton from your measurements and send it to you free. It is unlined and yours to keep, and it is where we settle the two things a waistcoat lives or dies by: length and the back.

Put it on with the trousers you will wear underneath and do up all but the bottom button. The hem should cover the waistband with no shirt showing, the armholes should sit close without cutting in, and the back should lie flat from collar to belt. Then sit down: a waistcoat that is too long rides up into a bubble, and one that is too tight pulls between the buttons. Tell us what you see, and whether you want lapels added.

## The Back of the Waistcoat: Satin, Belt and Buckle

Most people never look at the back of a vest until they take the jacket off at a wedding. The back of a waistcoat is usually made of lining rather than the front cloth, because it lives under a jacket: a smooth satin back slides under the jacket lining, stays cool and adds no bulk.

| Side slits | Short slits at the sides let the waistcoat move when you sit and bend |
| Full back waistcoat | A version with the back in the same wool as the front, for wearing without a jacket; ask us if you want one |

If you searched for a suit vest back or the back of a suit vest to see what it should look like, the photo above is ours: the satin panel, the darts that shape it to the small of the back, and the waistcoat back adjuster sitting just above the trouser waistband. A tuxedo vest back is built the same way. The waistcoat back design is simple on purpose: a dress vest back is satin from collar to hem, with the suit vest back strap and suit vest back buckle at the small of the back, and on the vest back side you see the slits that let it move. A formal waistcoat back side follows the same plan, and a full back vest, with wool on the back too, is the exception rather than the rule. Backless waistcoats, cut with only a strap behind the neck and waist, are a different garment and are not what we make here.

How It Should Fit

## How a Waistcoat Should Fit, Front and Back

How should a vest fit in the back? Flat and quiet. The back should skim your shoulder blades without ripples, the neckline should hug your shirt collar without gaping, and the hem at the back should be level with the front so no shirt shows above the trousers.

| Length | Covers the trouser waistband by about an inch, front and back |
| --- | --- |
| Front | Lies flat between the buttons; leave the bottom button undone |
| Armholes | Close to the body, so no shirt billows out under the arm when seen in a suit vest side view or any waistcoat side view |
| Back | No horizontal ripples or vertical bunching; the belt only fine-tunes it |

When people look at a vest from behind, a suit vest from the back, a mens vest back or a waistcoat from the back in photos, what they notice is wrinkling across the back or a gap at the collar. Both come from the pattern, not the buckle, which is why we check the back on your test waistcoat before cutting the wool.

Questions & Answers

## Questions About the Men’s Wool Waistcoat

### What is the back of a waistcoat made of?

Usually lining, not the front cloth. Ours has a satin back in the color you choose, which slides under a jacket without bulk, with a belt and buckle adjuster at the waist. A full back in wool is possible if you want to wear it without a jacket; ask us when you order.

### How should a vest fit in the back?

It should lie flat across your shoulder blades with no ripples, hug your shirt collar at the neck, and end level with the front so it covers your trouser waistband. The buckle is for fine adjustment; if the back still wrinkles, the pattern needs correcting, which is what the test waistcoat is for.

### How long should a waistcoat be?

Long enough to cover the trouser waistband, front and back, so no shirt shows between them. With low-rise trousers that means a longer waistcoat; we cut the length from your measurements and confirm it on the test waistcoat.

### Should I leave the bottom button undone?

Yes, by tradition the bottom button of a single-breasted waistcoat is left open. The pointed hem is cut to sit well that way, and it gives you room when you sit.

### Can I wear a wool waistcoat without a jacket?

Yes. With matching trousers, a shirt and tie it looks finished on its own, as in our photos. Keep it fitted and the right length, since without a jacket the waistcoat is the first thing people see.

### Can I match it to trousers or a suit?

Yes. It is cut from the same Super 160s Vitale Barberis Canonico worsted as our wool dress pants, so the two match exactly in the same color. For a jacket in the same cloth, ask us before ordering.

### Can I add lapels or a collar?

Yes, free of charge. Write it in the comment box on the measurement form, or email us with your order number. Lapels give the waistcoat a more formal, vintage look.

### What is the difference between a waistcoat and a vest?

Nothing but the word: a men’s waistcoat and a vest are the same garment. Waistcoat is the British term and vest the American one; in Britain a vest is an undershirt, which is why tailors there say waistcoat.

### Is the test waistcoat free, and how long does it take?

Yes, the test waistcoat is free and yours to keep. It takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch; once you confirm the fit, your final waistcoat takes 3 to 4 weeks to make and arrives 5 to 10 days after dispatch. Order as early as you can for a wedding.
